# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary users are Thai dog and cat owners who want practical, trustworthy guidance for daily care, food, behavior, and health-related questions. They often arrive with a specific concern, such as a symptom, feeding question, or care task, and need answers they can scan quickly before deciding what to do next. They expect information to feel complete and useful, but not chaotic or hard to read.

## Product Purpose

GoodPet exists to become a trusted Thai knowledge brand for dog and cat care. In phase 1, it is a read-only content experience that helps users find reliable, structured information through category hubs, search, and evergreen articles. Success means users can quickly understand what the site covers, find the right topic path, and feel confident that the information is carefully curated and responsibly presented.

## Brand Personality

Warm, trustworthy, contemporary.

The voice should feel calm, practical, and editorial rather than clinical, childish, or sales-driven. The emotional goal is to make visitors feel reassured, informed, and guided by a brand that takes pet knowledge seriously without sounding cold.

## Anti-references

- Hospital-like pet websites that feel cold, rigid, or intimidating
- Cute pet-shop aesthetics that weaken authority and seriousness
- Generic SaaS-style interfaces that feel like dashboards instead of media
- Empty, overly minimal Western-style landing pages with too little information
- Noisy portal-style content farms that prioritize density over clarity

## Design Principles

1. Build trust through structure first: category clarity, search, article hierarchy, and responsible medical framing should lead the experience.
2. Make dense information feel easy: the site should feel content-rich in an Asian-media sense, but organized enough to scan comfortably.
3. Let warmth come from tone and composition, not gimmicks: the brand should feel approachable without becoming playful or unserious.
4. Create a real editorial point of view: GoodPet should feel like a designed media brand, not a default CMS theme or component library demo.
5. Support long-term topical authority: every surface should reinforce clear dog and cat knowledge pathways that expand cleanly over time.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Phase 1 should aim for clear, readable, inclusive content with strong hierarchy and comfortable mobile reading. Prefer high-contrast text, obvious navigation, and scan-friendly layouts over decorative subtlety. Motion should remain restrained and respect reduced-motion preferences. Health-related content should include clear disclaimers that information does not replace professional veterinary diagnosis.
