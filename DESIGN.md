# Design System: Petster

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Trustworthy Companion Desk"**

Petster should feel like a well-kept editorial desk for modern pet owners: calm, useful, current, and visibly organized by someone who cares about getting the details right. The experience is not luxury-minimal and not portal-chaotic. It should feel information-rich in the way strong Asian content products do, but disciplined enough that a worried dog or cat owner can scan quickly and find a useful path without stress.

The design must communicate warmth without softness-for-its-own-sake. Petster is not a toy store, not a meme brand, and not a veterinary hospital portal. It should feel like a trusted media brand that knows how to structure knowledge, present nuance, and help people make better everyday decisions for their pets.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Medium-density editorial layout with clear grouping
- High scanability on mobile
- Calm authority, never cold authority
- Brand warmth carried by typography, color accents, and composition
- Dog and cat pathways that feel distinct, but still part of one brand system

## Colors

Petster should use a **restrained editorial palette** with one clear brand accent, clean architectural neutrals, and a smaller warm support accent used sparingly.

### Primary

- **Calm Teal-Blue** ([to be resolved during implementation]): the main brand color used for search emphasis, important links, selected states, and key calls to action. It should feel contemporary and steady, not loud, neon, or startup-generic.

### Secondary

- **Warm Coral Signal** ([to be resolved during implementation]): a supporting accent for small emphasis moments, badges, highlights, and occasional editorial punctuation. It exists to keep the brand human, not to dominate the interface.

### Neutral

- **Clean Light Background** ([to be resolved during implementation]): the main page canvas should be bright and uncluttered, with no beige AI-default warmth.
- **Soft Surface Neutral** ([to be resolved during implementation]): a subtle secondary surface for cards, sections, and grouped content areas.
- **Deep Ink** ([to be resolved during implementation]): the core reading color for headlines and body text. It must stay strong enough for long-form reading and health-related content.
- **Quiet Supporting Neutral** ([to be resolved during implementation]): for metadata, secondary labels, and supporting UI copy.

**The Warmth-Without-Beige Rule.** Warmth comes from composition, voice, and accent use. Do not rely on creamy, sandy, parchment-like backgrounds to create emotional tone.

**The Search-First Accent Rule.** The strongest accent should reinforce navigation and finding information, not decorative flourishes.

**Character:** Typography should carry much of the brand's personality. Headings should feel editorial and recognizable, while body text must remain practical, calm, and highly readable on mobile. Petster should not look like a default SaaS app, and it should not look like a nostalgic print magazine either.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** ([to be resolved during implementation]): used in hero moments and major editorial statements only. Large, confident, but not theatrical.
- **Headline** ([to be resolved during implementation]): used for section titles, article titles, and major content grouping.
- **Title** ([to be resolved during implementation]): used for cards, sub-sections, and structured content blocks.
- **Body** ([to be resolved during implementation]): optimized for Thai reading comfort, long-form scanning, and mobile clarity.
- **Label** ([to be resolved during implementation]): used for metadata, category tags, UI labels, and navigational support text.

**The Readability-First Rule.** Display typography may express the brand, but body typography must always win when the user is anxious, skimming, or reading on a phone.

**The No-Default-Tech-Type Rule.** Do not let the visual voice collapse into generic SaaS typography or overused AI-era font combinations.

## Elevation

Petster should use a **light layered system**, not a heavy card-shadow system. Depth should mostly come from spacing, section grouping, tonal contrast, and occasional subtle lift on interactive elements. Flat by default, slightly elevated on focus and interaction.

**The Structure-Before-Shadow Rule.** If a section does not read clearly without shadow, the layout is wrong. Fix hierarchy first.

## Components

### Buttons

- **Shape:** softly shaped, disciplined corners ([to be resolved during implementation])
- **Primary:** filled with the main brand color, clear contrast, and enough visual weight to feel trustworthy rather than playful
- **Hover / Focus:** subtle lift or emphasis, never glossy or over-animated
- **Secondary / Ghost:** quiet, editorial, and useful for navigation or supporting actions

### Search

- **Role:** search is a primary product surface, not a decorative hero prop
- **Style:** prominent, stable, and immediately understandable
- **Behavior:** should visually invite symptom questions, feeding questions, and daily care queries

### Category Blocks

- **Style:** easy to scan, editorial rather than dashboard-like
- **Purpose:** help people understand the site structure at a glance

### Article Cards

- **Style:** calm, content-first, and structured for fast scanning
- **Priority:** title, category, and practical value should be clearer than decoration

### Trust Section

- **Role:** communicates editorial policy, references, and responsible guidance
- **Style:** restrained and confident, not medicalized, not defensive

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** design for medium information density with clean grouping and strong content hierarchy.
- **Do** make search and topic pathways visually important from the first screen.
- **Do** use typography to create a real editorial identity instead of relying on decorative UI chrome.
- **Do** keep dog and cat content paths clear while preserving one shared brand language.
- **Do** optimize every surface for Thai reading comfort, especially on mobile.

### Don't:

- **Don't** make Petster look like a hospital-like pet website that feels cold, rigid, or intimidating.
- **Don't** drift into cute pet-shop aesthetics that weaken authority and seriousness.
- **Don't** use generic SaaS-style interfaces that feel like dashboards instead of media.
- **Don't** fall into empty, overly minimal Western-style landing pages with too little information.
- **Don't** imitate noisy portal-style content farms that prioritize density over clarity.
- **Don't** use beige AI-default warmth, purple-on-white AI gradients, or default shadcn-looking layouts as a shortcut to "modern."
